# 1. Rolling the Dice.
When a player proposes an action with uncertain outcomes and/or consequences, they roll a pool of d6s. The pool size equals the player character's act rating. For the result, read the highest die:

| ♦ Roll | Result                                                                              |
| -----: | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
|  1 - 3 | **Failure.** *The action falls through, or results in a negative consequence.*      |
|  4 - 5 | **Partial success.** *The action is resolved with a complication or a consequence.* |
|      6 | **Full success.** *The action is resolved with no complications.*                   |

Two or more dice with the result of 6 mean critical effects, which provide extra success, or unexpected positive consequences.
And if the player has no rating in given Act, they roll with a disadvantage - 2d6, and they read the lowest die.
Before a roll, players can choose to **push** their characters. The character takes 1 strain on each bar (see section 4) and the player either adds one die to their pool, or raises their Payoff (see section 2).

Certain abilities can grant extra dice to the pool. Dice are the only currency that touches the pool; there are no flat numeric modifiers. The GM and NPCs never roll

# 2. Peril & Payoff.
There are no turns, initiative, or measured movement in the game. There are also no mechanical rulings for travel, trading, or gear. The actions are resolved using the **Peril & Payoff** system. After the player chooses their Act, before they roll, the GM considers their situation, context like equipment, and informs them of their Peril and Payoff.

Peril is how risky an action is. It can be:
- **Trivial** - *A safe action with menial consequences.*
- **Hazardous** - *The default with considerable consequences.*
- **Grave** - *A dangerous action with severe consequences.*

The Payoff, on the other hand, is how effective a successful action could prove.
- **Poor** - *An outcome below what was desirable.*
- **Fruitful** - *The default, accomplishing your goal.*
- **Miraculous** - *A success far beyond what was intended.*

There is another condition for rolls - **Impossible**. The ruling comes in play when the GM believes an action is truly beyond accomplishment, and will only yield to a critical success. Impossible actions can only be attempted once, and always have Miraculous payoff. 

After being informed of their Peril & Payoff, the player can choose to change their approach, cancel the action, or deliberately accept worse Peril for a better Payoff, and vice versa.
These manipulations cannot be applied to Impossible rolls.

The player may also enlist a Gambit, which provides them one more die in their pool, with a guaranteed complication or consequence. 

The only exception to the peril & Payoff system is when two players are acting against each others interests. They roll as they normally would, and the player who rolls better takes the advantage.

# 3. Acts.
There are twelve Acts in the game, each belonging to one of four Traits.

|              | **Physical**                     | Quality      |
| -----------: | -------------------------------- | :----------- |
|    **Force** | *Move, bend, hurt.*              | ***Direct*** |
|   **Endure** | *Withstand, outlast, persist.*   | ***Still***  |
| **Maneuver** | *Evade, finesse, fool.*          | ***Agile***  |
|              |                                  |              |
|              | **Mental**                       |              |
|     **Hunt** | *Track, scan, outwit.*           | ***Direct*** |
|   **Reason** | *Analyze, recall, deduce.*       | ***Still***  |
|   **Tinker** | *Craft, devise, theorize.*       | ***Agile***  |
|              |                                  |              |
|              | **Spiritual**                    |              |
|     **Ruin** | *Annihilate, destroy, unmake.*   | ***Direct*** |
|     **Form** | *Conjure, create, summon.*       | ***Still***  |
|    **Alter** | *Warp, transmute, change.*       | ***Agile***  |
|              |                                  |              |
|              | **Social**                       |              |
|     **Sway** | *Persuade, charm, seduce.*       | ***Direct*** |
|  **Command** | *Intimidate, lead, impose.*      | ***Still***  |
|  **Perform** | *Captivate, express, embellish.* | ***Agile***  |

Each Act holds a rating between 0 and 5. That rating is your dice pool while performing the given Act.

Every time the players perform an Act at grave Peril, they mark 1 xp for that Act's Trait.
After a session ends, players also mark 1 xp (for their character or Trait) if they've met the following conditions:
- *If they've addressed a challenge with their heritage's or archetype's strengths.*
- *If they've rolled a critical success.*
- *If they've performed a group action.*

There are two experience tracks - Trait xp and character xp.
6 marks in Trait xp result in said Trait leveling up. The player ranks one of the Trait's Acts up.
8 marks in character xp result in the character leveling up. The player gains 1♥ toward an ability.

# 4. Stopwatches.
Stopwatches track progress toward a goal or an imposing danger. The GM draws a circle, divides it into segments, and fills them in as play moves toward (or away from) the development it represents. When all segments are filled, the development occurs.

The number of segments reflects the Stopwatch's complexity. The Payoff of a roll determines how many segments a success fills: A full success fills the segments suggested by the Payoff; a partial success fills fewer and adds a complication; a failure leaves the Stopwatch untouched, or ticks an opposing danger instead.

There are no set numbers for exactly how far a success progresses a stopwatch, since some stopwatches may be simple in nature but complex in execution. The offered template is that Miraculous success fills 3-4 segments, while a poor success could fill maybe 1.

Stopwatches may also progress independent of the players, with time passing in the narrative itself.

Often a goal Stopwatch is paired against a danger Stopwatch. A partial success can fill both at once: progress and a complication, together.

# 5. Flesh, Soul and Damage.
Characters carry two strain bars - **Flesh♠** and **Soul♣**. Each ranges from 0 to 10.

The bars start empty and fill as you exert yourself. As a bar fills, crossing a threshold **damages** you with strain. Strain damage is cleared when the strain is lowered below its threshold. The two bars share one set of damage slots.

- 5 and up - you take **minor** damage.
- 8 and up - you take **moderate** damage.
- 10 - you take **major** damage.

The GM may never directly touch a player's strain - only through status ailments.

The thresholds fire every time you land on them, so being on 7 strain would leave the player with 3 additional instances of minor strain damage. Damage is written into a slot as a specific condition. Strain damage is acquired and cleared with raising and lowering strain, while consequence damage is acquired through bad rolls and narrative, and is cleared through abilities, items, and other means. There are three minor damage slots, two moderate damage slots, and one major damage slot.
- **Minor damage** - *reduced Payoff on impeded Acts.*
- **Moderate damage** - *one less die to impeded rolls.*
- **Major damage** - *you cannot Act without help.*

If damage would be written but every slot of that tier is full, it escalates to the next tier up. Damage past the Major slot kills the character. 
### Resisting:
When the GM states a consequence, the player may choose to **resist** it. Resistance always works - the consequence is reduced or negated - but the effort costs strain. Roll the **Trait** matching the consequence's type (Physical, Mental, Spiritual or Social, counting one die per non-zero Act in that Trait) and read the highest:
- **Failure:** *+3 Strain.*
- **Partial Success:** *+2 Strain.*
- **Full Success:** *+1 Strain.*
- **Critical Success:** *+0 Strain.*

The strain lands on the bar tied to that Trait.
- **Physical** and **Social** consequences strain **Flesh♠**.
- **Mental** and **Spiritual** consequences strain **Soul♣**.

Resisting only ever raises strain, save where an archetype allows otherwise. Only rest and recuperation can lower it.
If one is to resist with an empty trait, they follow disadvantage rules - 2d6, and read the lowest.
### Rest:
Rest serves to clear strain, and it occurs automatically during narrative intervals, when the player is eating, meditating, sleeping, praying, or otherwise at peace for a notable period of time. There's four types of rest:
- **Respite,** which recovers 2 strain in each bar. Momentary breather after a taxing occasion.
- **Relief,** which recovers 4 strain in each bar. Satisfaction felt after a hearty meal, or a calming prayer.
- **Repose,** which recovers 6 strain in each bar. Sleep, pleasure, recreation.
- **Relaxation,** which clears strain entirely, and moves all damage down one tier. Several days of peace.
### Recuperation:
Recuperation is a type of roll a player can make to trigger rest manually. The player rolls a 2d6 and reads the highest die, with standard rules.
- **Failure:** *No change, wasted action.*
- **Partial Success:** *Recover 1 strain in each bar.*
- **Full Success:** *Recover 3 strain in each bar.*
- **Critical Success:** *Recover 5 strain in each bar.*

When a character is slain, they can be revived using magic, or by being delivered to a place of faith, but a revived character loses one level, by sacrificing an ability, or an act rank.

# 6. Sequences and Group Actions.
During play, a series of rolls may be formalized as a **Sequence**. Each consecutive successful roll in a sequence gives opportunity for another, but taking more than one action in a sequence costs a player 1 strain per the roll's Trait. A failed roll during a sequence, among other consequences, will interrupt the sequence.

A player may begin a sequence alone, or with other players, which would turn it into a **Group Action.**

There are three types of Group Actions: **Help**, **Unite**, and **Group Sequence**.
### Help:
When a player is rolling, they may enlist **Help** from another. If the player agrees, they apply one strain in each bar, and lend another dice to their pool.
### Unite:
Two or more players may join forces to perform the same Act. They roll their combined pool together, and read the highest as one otherwise would. One cannot Unite in Resisting, only in Acting. If a player has 0 rank in the act, they may **Help**, instead. United actions cannot produce critical successes, and they cost each participant 1 strain of the Act's Trait.
### Group Sequence:
Two or more players may initiate a group sequence, in which they plan out a combined plan of action, and roll as the fiction demands. If one player fails an action, the sequence may end, or another participant may choose to **Help** them post-factum. Post-factum help is only available during Group Sequences.
**Help** and **Unite** may be part of a Group Sequence, and a Sequence at Grave Peril only yields 2 xp total, instead of 1 xp per roll.

If a Group Action ends in partial success or failure, all or only some parties involved may suffer the consequences, dictated by the fiction.

# 7. Archetypes, Heritage and Abilities.
Every character has one archetype. It does not restrict which abilities they can learn - abilities are an open pool - but it shapes what the character starts good at and how they endure. Each archetype comes with two **pre-boosted Acts**, granting a free starting rank in each that no other archetype provides, along with a unique quirk.

Each of the thirteen classes holds abilities. Abilities are not tied to your archetype - anyone may learn any of them. They come in three kinds:
- **Passive** - *always in effect once owned.*
- **Active** - *triggered with a roll when you choose to use them.*
- **Strenuous** - *Active, with a price: using them costs you strain, forcing you to resist.*
Abilities have pre-determined traits they require, with the player rolling one of its acts, and the resistance, accordingly.

Characters also have heritage, which determines their general appearance, grants them some kind of passive race-specific property, and two acts that will be their strengths. 
### Unlocking abilities:
Abilities are bought with ♥ (see section 3).

- An ability from a class **within your archetype** and costs **1♥**.
- An ability from a class **outside your archetype** costs **2♥**.

A character may own at most 12 abilities.

# 8. Spells, Scrolls and Attunement.
Spells in the game are another kind of action that players can take. Unlike abilities, spells have pre-determined Acts they require to roll. Each spell also has a cast limit, and some stronger spells may also require a Stopwatch to be filled (see section 4).

Spells typically take up space in player inventories as scrolls. However, if a player has a full spell scroll, they can attempt to **Attune** it.

**Attunement** is a type of roll - of the Trait that the spell's Act requires - which, if successful, permanently binds the spell to the player, allowing the spell to be recharged back to the limit after rest.
If partially successful, the binding fails, but the scroll remains intact.
And, if the attunement fails, the scroll is consumed and needs to be recharged through narrative means.
An attunement roll also inflicts strain the way a Resistance roll does.

When the player has exhausted the cast limit on an attuned spell, they may take 3 strain of its Trait to cast it again.

A character may at most attune 10 spells at the same time.

# 9. Quests.
as the narrative progresses, the GM may persistently draw attention to potential storylines, which will be referred to as Quests. Quests may enter table as the GM seems fit, and they only leave it if the world itself doesn't allow for them anymore, or, if they are completed - successfully or otherwise.

Quests are made up of the following elements, clearly communicated to the players:
- **Location** - Where the specific quest line can be initiated.
- **Keys** - Which specific characters, items or conditions are required to initiate it.
- **Runtime** - How long a quest is expected to last, measured in sessions, per table.
- **Experience** - The amount of XP a quest upon completion, per character.

After a quest is initiated, it also gains a status field. Quests may be ongoing, closed, or stalled. 

Emergently, the players may initiate a quest without formally doing so, or sometimes even knowing a quest existed in the first place. An emergent quest, once initiated, gains a writ entry on the table alongside other quests, revealing its four fields. Emergent quests exist to leave a visible imprint upon play, and to prevent the players from feeling like writ quests are ever their only available paths forward.

Like stopwatches, the Quest system exists to communicate to the players potential storylines and journeys they can be embarking upon, if they are wondering what to do next, or if their previous quests happen to be stalled.

Quests also assume that not all player characters will be present for every session, and that certain characters may miss certain events. This is not only allowed, but in case of high player count tables - even encouraged.

# 10. Character Creation.
Upon creating a character, pick the name, the archetype and the heritage.

Then, mark your archetype's and heritage's Act ranks. Repeat act ranks add up.

Then, pick any ability from the wheel for free, and fill its ♥ on the sheet.

Finally, you have two points to unlock an ability, raise an Act's rank, or one of each.
