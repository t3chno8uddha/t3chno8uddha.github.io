There are no turns, initiative, or measured movement in the game. There are also no mechanical rulings for travel, trading, or gear. The actions are resolved using the **Peril & Payoff** system. After the player chooses their Act, before they roll, the GM considers their situation, context like equipment, and informs them of their Peril and Payoff.

Peril is how risky an action is. It can be:
- **Trivial** - *A safe action with menial consequences.*
- **Hazardous** - *The default with considerable consequences.*
- **Grave** - *A dangerous action with severe consequences.*

The Payoff, on the other hand, is how effective a successful action could prove.
- **Poor** - *An outcome below what was desirable.*
- **Fruitful** - *The default, accomplishing your goal.*
- **Miraculous** - *A success far beyond what was intended.*

There is another condition for rolls - **Impossible**. The ruling comes in play when the GM believes an action is truly beyond accomplishment, and will only yield to a critical success. Impossible actions can only be attempted once, and always have Miraculous payoff. 

After being informed of their Peril & Payoff, the player can choose to change their approach, cancel the action, or deliberately accept worse Peril for a better Payoff, and vice versa.
These manipulations cannot be applied to Impossible rolls.

The player may also enlist a Gambit, which provides them one more die in their pool, with a guaranteed complication or consequence. 

The only exception to the peril & Payoff system is when two players are acting against each others interests. They roll as they normally would, and the player who rolls better takes the advantage.
