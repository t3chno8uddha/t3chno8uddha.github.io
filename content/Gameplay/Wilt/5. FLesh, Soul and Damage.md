Characters carry two strain bars - **Flesh♠** and **Soul♣**. Each ranges from 0 to 10.

The bars start empty and fill as you exert yourself. As a bar fills, crossing a threshold **damages** you with strain. Strain damage is cleared when the strain is lowered below its threshold. The two bars share one set of damage slots.

- 5 and up - you take **minor** damage.
- 8 and up - you take **moderate** damage.
- 10 - you take **major** damage.

The GM may never directly touch a player's strain - only through status ailments.

The thresholds fire every time you land on them, so being on 7 strain would leave the player with 3 additional instances of minor strain damage. Damage is written into a slot as a specific condition. Strain damage is acquired and cleared with raising and lowering strain, while consequence damage is acquired through bad rolls and narrative, and is cleared through abilities, items, and other means. There are three minor damage slots, two moderate damage slots, and one major damage slot.
- **Minor damage** - *reduced Payoff on impeded Acts.*
- **Moderate damage** - *one less die to impeded rolls.*
- **Major damage** - *you cannot Act without help.*

If damage would be written but every slot of that tier is full, it escalates to the next tier up. Damage past the Major slot kills the character. 
### Resisting:
When the GM states a consequence, the player may choose to **resist** it. Resistance always works - the consequence is reduced or negated - but the effort costs strain. Roll the **Trait** matching the consequence's type (Physical, Mental, Spiritual or Social, counting one die per non-zero Act in that Trait) and read the highest:
- **Failure:** *+3 Strain.*
- **Partial Success:** *+2 Strain.*
- **Full Success:** *+1 Strain.*
- **Critical Success:** *+0 Strain.*

The strain lands on the bar tied to that Trait.
- **Physical** and **Social** consequences strain **Flesh♠**.
- **Mental** and **Spiritual** consequences strain **Soul♣**.

Resisting only ever raises strain, save where an archetype allows otherwise. Only rest and recuperation can lower it.
If one is to resist with an empty trait, they follow disadvantage rules - 2d6, and read the lowest.
### Rest:
Rest serves to clear strain, and it occurs automatically during narrative intervals, when the player is eating, meditating, sleeping, praying, or otherwise at peace for a notable period of time. There's four types of rest:
- **Respite,** which recovers 2 strain in each bar. Momentary breather after a taxing occasion.
- **Relief,** which recovers 4 strain in each bar. Satisfaction felt after a hearty meal, or a calming prayer.
- **Repose,** which recovers 6 strain in each bar. Sleep, pleasure, recreation.
- **Relaxation,** which clears strain entirely, and moves all damage down one tier. Several days of peace.
### Recuperation:
Recuperation is a type of roll a player can make to trigger rest manually. The player rolls a 2d6 and reads the highest die, with standard rules.
- **Failure:** *No change, wasted action.*
- **Partial Success:** *Recover 1 strain in each bar.*
- **Full Success:** *Recover 3 strain in each bar.*
- **Critical Success:** *Recover 5 strain in each bar.*

When a character is slain, they can be revived using magic, or by being delivered to a place of faith, but a revived character loses one level, by sacrificing an ability, or an act rank.

It is important to note that reviving a character doesn't heal them - it merely ensouls them. If the character's body was injured, they remain injured upon revival, unless healed prior to it.
