Edicts are special kinds of spells, most commonly associated with [[Abara]]. They function in an equational, rule-based manner, and said rules are self-governing across individual edicts. Declaring an Edict takes a permanent toll on the caster's soul.

Edicts come in two kinds - Enchantments, and Execrations. They are the solar and lunar counterparts to each other. The main difference between the two is when the price on one's soul is paid - while the edict is cast, like in case of Enchantments, or after its violation, like in case of execrations. The gods are said to frown upon Execrations, often barring those who would cast them from entering their afterlives. Souls with active execrations cast cannot be revived, either.

Edicts are the most uncharted of magics, establishing them less as the arcane and more as the metaphysical. In that sense, Edicts are like magical amendments to the law of the world itself. Edicts can never be longer than one sentence.

An example would be the execration [[Rafael]] Tannengard put on his brother, [[Raul]] - one that declared, to summarize:

>*Raul's power and memories are to be sealed within his limbs, scattered across time and space.*

By regaining his limbs, Raul would contradict a declaration of his execration, and its surrounding conditions would break as well: His limbs being reunited with him would mean his memories and powers resurfacing, as well.
