The party finds themselves camping the night out, sitting beside a fire. The starry night shines equally bright on all of you, and the crescent moon almost looks like a smile. There is food cooking in a pot above the campfire, and Francis tends to the meal. The horses, close by, munch on grass and hay, and the Aél Theo has left the party. Hemos finally walks out of the caravan to meet the new characters, and so does [[Graphel]].
![[Camping_Night.gif]]
Some banter later, the party is passed by Raul, who announces he's headed east, and needs a port city, one like Madina Porro. During the conversation, he also points out that Graphel's book is glowing, and upon touching it, Graphel becomes witness to a messy future that awaits Asphodia. Him and his party members dying as some deities tower over them, and he hears a few names, only one of which he could make out - Duke Daedalus.

Raul assures him that it's probably pixies playing tricks on him and that they should get 
moving, come dawn. So the dawn comes and they head to the nearest city north - 
[[Asbarnia]].

The liveliness of this place is a far cry from most of what came before; Flourishing greenery crawls all over its stone and wood structures, with foliage around every corner. Every single breath you draw feels more invigorating than the last, as if all your worries left your lungs alongside your exhales. A curly river makes for the town's veins, splitting several times across the way, and gondolas can be seen almost floating on the river like airborne feathers. The townsfolk are varied - most, Gail, some Ael ones, and a few Undine can even be spotted by the gondolas. No hatred to be seen in anyone's eyes. Some teenagers run amok, causing minor, harmless disturbance. One of them bumps into Francis, collects his scattered things and runs back towards his friends.
The party settle down, find a place to sleep and eat, and Graphel notices a building with a similar logo to the one on the amulet he found - another library.
They enter the small wardrobe which opens up into a massive structure inside - one of extravagance and luxury. All kinds of folks walk around here, some of which with resentment rendered on their faces, others - complete apathy. The arch librarian greets the party and explains that the arch libraries offer hidden, peaceful communities to sorcerers alike to share their knowledge. He asks how the party managed to enter. The arch librarian does not know anything about Graphel's book, not in its fairy tale state, anyway. "Seems to be well-written," he says, after merely running his hand over it. 

	"And well kept, too. The handwriting seems to be northern, is that why you're headed that way? Handwritings are like accents, and this "accent" seems too ancient, I can't assure you that whoever you look for is still alive and present. Do prey tell, however, can you think of anything unique about this particular book?"
	
After Graphel explains, the arch librarian states that it could have something to do with Kronitia.
	
	"The merciless demigodess Cronika, among other things, is the keeper of time. It is said that she kept two books, one for the past, and one for the future. Son, if what this book showed you was indeed the future, then it may be the case that Cronika chose you, and this book, for a greater purpose."

Once the party leave, someone else bumps into Graphel. As thoughts of how clumsy this populace is, he hears the man mumble something to his partner, involving the name "Duke Daedalus." That man is the town's bishop Clairmont, who looks comically evil, refuses to honor anyone from the party with a seance, considering "how busy he happens to be." Raul, after thinking it over, if no one else suggests it, offers the party that may be they should pay the bishop's mansion a visit.
For a holy man's residence, there are one too many of his guards stationed all around the mansion. The characters have to be sneaky, in order to not get themselves arrested, banished or even executed.
